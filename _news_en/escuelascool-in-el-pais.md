On 18 November 2026, **EL PAÍS Chile** published the op-ed *"El cambio climático también profundiza la desigualdad educativa"* (Climate change is also deepening educational inequality), written by **Martín Andrade Ruiz-Tagle**, Executive Director of Corporación Ciudades and co-investigator on EscuelasCool.

The piece argues that extreme heat is already part of the school experience of millions of students across Latin America. Poorly ventilated classrooms, playgrounds without shade and schools surrounded by concrete harm the health of students and teachers, as well as their learning. Drawing on data from the IDB, UNICEF and studies from Peru and Brazil, the article shows that these effects are not evenly shared: they hit schools and communities with fewer resources hardest. In Greater Santiago, analyses by Corporación Ciudades identify municipalities such as Quilicura, Cerro Navia, Conchalí and Cerrillos, where every neighbourhood exceeds the average land surface temperature.

With a possible new El Niño episode in 2027, the op-ed calls for moving beyond a reactive response and adapting schools to the climate ahead: reviewing school timetables and calendars, putting protocols for extreme temperatures in place, improving ventilation, adding shade and vegetation, transforming playgrounds and integrating schools into city planning, including as climate refuges for their communities.

The article describes EscuelasCool as a Wellcome-funded initiative that brings together six institutions from Chile, Colombia and Peru and, led by Yasna Palmeiro-Silva, generates evidence on the effects of extreme heat on health, wellbeing and learning in order to translate it into better education and urban policy decisions. Corporación Ciudades takes part in the project through its Barrios por el Clima (Neighbourhoods for the Climate) programme.
